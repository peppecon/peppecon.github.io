Welcome!

I am a fifth-year Ph.D. student in Economics at **Bocconi University** in Milan.

This Spring semester, I will be a Visiting Student Researcher at the Stanford Economics Department, hosted by [Luigi Bocola](https://lbocola.people.stanford.edu/).

My research focuses on how workers sort across jobs and the consequences for human capital and wages. I am particularly interested in how uncertainty shapes the behaviour of firms and workers and how those responses affect the aggregate economy.

**Research interests**: Macroeconomics, Labor Economics, and Structural Econometrics.

## Working Papers

<div class="abstract-container">
  <div class="abstract-header">
    <span class="arrow">▶</span>
    <span>"The Micro and Macro Implications of Multidimensional Skill Uncertainty"</span>
  </div>
  <div class="abstract-content">
    <div class="abstract-expanded">
      <div class="abstract-text">
What are the consequences of multidimensional skill uncertainty for workers' wages and aggregate output? I develop and estimate a general equilibrium dynamic Roy model in which workers have imperfect information about their multidimensional skills and accumulate task-specific human capital. Estimated on Portuguese administrative data, the model implies that removing information frictions about skills would raise aggregate output by 5.2%. These gains are highly unequal across workers: the worst-matched within occupation would earn over 20% more under full information, while the best-matched would earn about 3% more. I then evaluate how much of these gains a feasible information policy can deliver. Calibrated to the treatment effect of a field experiment, a skill assessment delivered at labor-market entry raises aggregate output by 0.91%, but its value decays sharply when the same assessment is delivered later in the career. Finally, job transformation induced by Large Language Model adoption raises long-run output, and raises the output gain available from removing information frictions from 5.2% to 5.9%.
