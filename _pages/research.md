## Working Papers

<div class="abstract-container">
  <div class="abstract-header">
    <span class="arrow">▶</span>
    <span>"The Micro and Macro Implications of Multidimensional Skill Uncertainty"</span>
  </div>
  <div class="abstract-content">
    <div class="abstract-expanded">
      <div class="abstract-text">
What are the consequences of multidimensional skill uncertainty for individual wages and aggregate output? How does it affect occupational reallocation to technology shocks? To answer these questions, I develop a general equilibrium dynamic Roy model in which workers have imperfect information about their multidimensional skills and accumulate task-specific human capital on the job. Estimating the model on Portuguese administrative data, I find that information frictions generates a 3.8% aggregate output loss. I then quantify how much of this loss can be recovered by giving workers information about their own skills: a calibrated skill assessment delivered at labor-market entry raises aggregate output by 1.05%, and its effectiveness decays sharply when the same assessment is delivered later in the career. Finally, I study the effects of job transformation induced by Large Language Model adoption. The shock raises long-run output and welfare at every adoption speed, but faster adoption front-loads the output gains at the cost of a sharp welfare loss for the cohorts caught mid-career; crucially, skill uncertainty becomes more costly, as the output and welfare gaps to full information widen along the transition.
